# How the bank account management system works

This guide follows a branch from the moment the manager opens the vault for the day to the point when the day's accounts are closed. It explains who handles each decision, where the money goes, and what staff can do if a transaction or approval cannot go ahead. All amounts in the examples are illustrative Myanmar kyat (MMK).

## Who does what

| Role | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Manager | Opens the business day, assigns cash to tellers, reviews customer identity, maintains account products and charges, manages staff access, approves expenses and corrections, and authorizes closing. |
| Officer | Registers customers, opens and maintains accounts, takes deposits, pays withdrawals and processes transfers. An officer acting as a teller must accept responsibility for the cash assigned to them. |
| Auditor | Reviews records, runs checks and investigates assigned differences. An auditor can also receive physical cash from a teller and arrange its return to a designated manager. |
| Customer | Supplies identity and account-opening information, provides any required documents, and gives instructions such as the recipient of a transfer or what should happen when a fixed deposit matures. Staff enter and process those instructions. |

**A staff login and a teller assignment serve different purposes.** Signing in gives an employee access to the work allowed for their role. Accepting a teller assignment identifies the person responsible for that day's cash. An officer can register a customer before accepting a till, but needs an active till to take an opening cash deposit.

A manager creates staff accounts, assigns roles, resets passwords and can withdraw access. New staff and staff whose password has been reset must change it before carrying out banking work. Five failed sign-in attempts cause a five-minute lock. The bank must retain at least one active manager.

## Opening the branch for the day

1. **Enter the vault cash.** The manager opens today's Myanmar business date and enters the actual starting amount in the vault. Zero is valid. The manager may save a preferred opening amount to fill in the form, then check it against the cash for that day.
2. **Assign tellers now or later.** For each assignment, the manager selects an active officer and enters the start time, end time and starting cash. Both times must fall on that date, the end must follow the start, and each teller's allocation must be positive. Cash amounts allow up to two decimal places.
3. **Confirm the opening.** The vault must have enough available cash for all assignments together. If an entry is invalid, the manager corrects it before the day and its assignments are opened. A manager can also open the day with no teller assignments.
4. **Accept the till.** The assigned officer accepts during the stated time window. Until then, the allocation is set aside in the vault. Acceptance moves it into the officer's till. An officer with another open till, or one still awaiting handover, must finish that assignment first. A missed assignment expires and the manager can schedule another.

**Example.** The vault opens with 1,000,000 MMK. The manager sets aside 200,000 for one officer. Before the officer accepts, all 1,000,000 is still in the vault, but only 800,000 is free to assign. After acceptance, the vault holds 800,000 and the officer is responsible for 200,000.

An earlier date can remain open for investigation and closing while the branch serves customers on today's date. Opening today does not close yesterday or erase yesterday's outstanding work.

## Registering customers and opening accounts

**Registration and identity review.** An officer or manager records the customer's name, birth date and identity details. The minimum age is 18. Citizens provide an NRC number (national registration card number), and foreign customers provide a passport number. Staff check for duplicate identity details and attach documents to the correct customer. A manager reviews the information and approves or rejects the identity check, also called KYC. An account can be opened only after every holder has been verified.

**Choosing the account.** The officer selects a product available to the customer. Products can have different opening deposits, minimum balances, referrer requirements and eligibility conditions. Some require the customer to hold another product first. An individual account has one owner. A joint account needs two different verified holders, one primary holder, ownership shares adding to 100%, and an instruction on how the holders sign. Where referrers are required, they must be distinct existing customers who already hold accounts.

**Completing the opening.** Staff supply each document required for the product. An eligible, unexpired document already held for either account holder can be copied into the account record. If that copy is unavailable, the officer supplies the document before proceeding. A positive opening deposit is received through the officer's active till. A product that permits a zero opening balance can be opened without receiving cash. Successful opening creates the account number and records its owners, documents and any opening deposit together.

**Changes after opening.** An authorized officer can review the statement and status history, update the existing joint holders' shares and signing instructions, or freeze, suspend or reactivate an eligible account. Changing the owners is outside a joint-holder edit. Frozen and suspended accounts cannot send or receive funds until reactivated. An eligible teller transaction can reactivate a dormant account. A closed account is final.

## Deposits, withdrawals and transfers

| Customer instruction | What staff do and what happens next |
| --- | --- |
| Deposit cash | The officer checks that the product accepts deposits and receives the cash. The customer's balance and the cash expected in the officer's till both increase. |
| Withdraw cash | The officer checks the available balance, the product's minimum balance and withdrawal limits, and the cash available in the till. A successful withdrawal reduces both the customer's balance and the till cash. |
| Transfer between accounts at the bank | The officer selects two different eligible accounts. The money leaves the sender and reaches the recipient together. An internal transfer is fee-free when the accounts share a holder. |
| Transfer to another bank | The officer selects the bank and enters the beneficiary and amount. The sender is charged and the transfer remains pending. An authorized officer or manager later records settlement or failure. Failure produces a linked refund of the principal and original fee. |
| Send money for NRC pickup | The sender may pay from an account or in cash. Staff record the receiver and the branch or other bank where payment is due. At a branch, staff check identity and pickup code before paying cash or crediting an eligible account. For another bank, staff record that bank's payout. |

A transfer can be pending even though the sender has already paid. Staff can look up its status, references and any refund in the transaction history. For a branch pickup, an eligible pending code can be reissued if it expires. Repeated incorrect code or identity checks can block pickup. Once the attempt limit is reached, staff must cancel and refund the transfer to the original funding method.

**Example — a transfer to another bank fails.** A customer with 300,000 MMK sends 100,000. Suppose this product charges 100 plus 1% of the amount, caps the fee at 800, and applies 5% tax to the capped fee. The fee before the cap is 1,100. After the cap and 40 in tax, the customer pays 840. Their balance falls to **199,160 MMK** while the transfer is pending. If staff record a failure, the bank records a refund of **100,840 MMK**. With no other activity, the balance returns to **300,000 MMK**, and both the original payment and the refund remain visible.

## Savings, fixed deposits and fees

The manager maintains product terms and the dates when interest rates and charges apply. Staff select from the available products when opening an account. A savings product can pay different rates for different balance ranges. For an eligible full month, the bank calculates savings interest from the qualifying balance and annual rate divided by twelve. If no rate covers the balance, that month earns no accrual. An accrual is interest earned and recorded for later payment.

For a **fixed deposit**, the customer chooses a term and tells the officer what to do at maturity. Staff also identify an eligible payout account held individually by the primary holder. Ordinary teller deposits, withdrawals and transfers cannot move money into or out of the fixed deposit. The customer can update eligible payout or renewal instructions before settlement.

| Customer's maturity instruction | What happens at the end of the term |
| --- | --- |
| Pay everything out | The principal and interest go to the payout account. The deposit and its account close. |
| Renew the principal | The interest goes to the payout account. The principal starts a new term. |
| Renew the principal and interest | The full balance starts a new term. |

If the payout account is unavailable, maturity processing waits until it can be used. For a withdrawal before maturity, a manager obtains a quote showing how much interest the customer keeps and how much will be given up. Completing the withdrawal pays the net amount to the payout account and closes the deposit.

**Example — early withdrawal.** On a principal of 365,000 MMK, 30 days at 10% per year earns 3,000 MMK using a 365-day year. If the early-withdrawal rate is 4%, the customer keeps 1,200 of that interest and gives up 1,800. The payout is **366,200 MMK**. Actual amounts depend on the customer's contract and the withdrawal date.

Maintenance charges can build up monthly and be collected at quarter end. Dormant-account penalties can be collected monthly. If there are insufficient funds, the unpaid charge remains recorded for later collection.

## Handling branch cash and expenses

**Asking for more cash.** An officer with an open till sends a request with a note and may state the amount needed. The manager chooses the amount to approve. Approval sets that money aside in the vault. When the officer confirms receipt, responsibility moves to the till. Rejection releases reserved cash. Officers can also transfer cash between eligible tills, with the same amount recorded as leaving one and entering the other.

**Recording an expense.** The requester identifies the till or vault, amount, payee and purpose, and supplies a receipt or explains why one is missing. The officer uses their own open till. A manager can submit a vault expense. A manager records a review note and approves or rejects the request. Approval records the expense and reduces expected cash. The requester can approve their own expense only if they are the sole active manager.

**Handing over at the end of a shift.** The officer counts the cash actually present. If the count is zero, the till can close immediately. If cash remains, the officer selects a recipient. While the till awaits acceptance, the officer cannot continue taking payments or submit another count. Acceptance closes the till. Decline reopens it so the officer can count again and submit a fresh handover.

| Who receives the cash? | What that means for the branch |
| --- | --- |
| Another eligible officer | Cash passes to the receiving officer's till on the same date. Both sides of the transfer are recorded. |
| A manager | The vault receives the amount the officer counted. Any difference from the amount expected stays with the original till for investigation. |
| An auditor | The auditor takes responsibility for the cash. To return it, the auditor selects whole handovers for one open date and names the receiving manager. Only that manager can accept the return into the vault. Decline leaves the cash with the auditor. |

## Investigating differences and closing the day

**Expected cash** is what the branch records say should be present. **Counted cash** is what a person actually found. Staff retain both figures so a manager can distinguish a counting mistake from missing money or a transaction recorded against the wrong till.

1. **Find the difference.** Staff compare customer transactions, the bank's accounting records, and the cash in each position. A reviewer assigns a finding to an investigator.
2. **Explain what happened.** The investigator checks the original transaction, count and handover. A counting mistake calls for a corrected count. An omitted withdrawal, duplicated cash movement, wrong customer account, missing accounting entry or genuine shortage each calls for a different remedy. The investigator gives the reason and supporting evidence and reviews the proposed result.
3. **Approve and check.** A manager approves or rejects the specific proposal. Normally another manager reviews a manager's own proposal. Self-approval requires a sole active manager with approval authority. The branch retains the original records. If the final check still fails, staff reopen the evidence and repeat the check without moving the money a second time.

**Example — from account opening to the final vault count.** An officer starts with 200,000 MMK, then receives a verified customer's opening deposit of 50,000. The customer has 50,000 in their account and the officer's till should contain 250,000. At handover the officer enters 245,000. The manager's receipt transfers 245,000 to the vault and leaves a **5,000 difference on the till**. If both parties establish that 250,000 was physically delivered, an approved count correction moves the 5,000 difference to the vault. The customer's deposit stays at 50,000. If only 245,000 was delivered, staff investigate the shortage. Recording a higher count would conceal it.

**Normal close.** The manager records the final physical vault count after outstanding custody, funding and expense decisions are settled. Staff review the day's checks and outstanding findings. An authorized reviewing manager approves, and the branch closes the date after a final check. A pending interbank transfer or NRC pickup can settle on a later date. Its pending status alone does not prevent closing.

| What prevents normal closure? | Who needs to act? |
| --- | --- |
| A till is open or cash is awaiting handover | Officer counts the till and the designated recipient accepts. If declined, the officer counts again. |
| An auditor still holds cash | Auditor requests the return and the designated manager confirms physical receipt. Submitting the request alone leaves the cash with the auditor. |
| Cash is reserved or an expense awaits review | The responsible officer or manager completes the receipt, approval, rejection or eligible cancellation. |
| A correction was approved but its result still fails the check | The assigned investigator or approving manager reviews the outstanding difference and repeats verification. |
| The final vault count is missing or an accounting difference remains | Manager records the count. Staff investigate the identified difference and complete the review. |

**Exceptional decisions.** A manager can record a reason for accepting a specific finding as it stands. If the underlying evidence changes, the finding can return for investigation. A separate decision, **force close**, closes the date despite outstanding issues. It requires a reason and agreement from every manager designated for that decision. The closing record keeps the outstanding issues. Later financial corrections use a later active business date. In particular, the usual auditor cash-return process requires the original date to remain open, so managers should settle that custody before deciding to force close.

## Reports, monthly work and service limits

Staff can follow a customer transaction from its account statement to the bank's accounting entries. Managers and auditors can inspect the bank's day and month summaries. Managers can look up interest earned, fees and fixed-deposit maturities. Audit history records who made a decision and when. Staff receive notices about work requiring their attention. Reading a notice still leaves the decision for the responsible person.

The bank checks for overdue teller assignments and due fixed deposits automatically. At monthly closing it checks that the prior period is closed, processes maintenance and interest, and prepares the monthly accounting summary. If an earlier business date is still open, the manager completes that day's closing before requesting another monthly run. Where an old account-opening deposit is missing only from the teller's cash history, an authorized repair can restore that link after the original transaction and till are identified. It does not charge the customer again.

Two limits matter when planning branch work. Staff can view accounting reports, but the accounting Excel export is unavailable in the version described here. For transfers involving another bank, staff record the settlement or payout outcome in this system. The branch still needs confirmation from the other bank before treating the external payment as complete.
